Guide: Dynamic Countdown Timer



Dynamic Countdown Timers let you create personalized countdown experiences based on when a contact receives or opens an email. Instead of showing every recipient the same deadline, Dynamic Timers can provide each contact with their own countdown window. You can also control whether the timer expires strictly after a configured duration or aligns with a specific end time.

What is a Dynamic Countdown Timer?


Dynamic Countdown Timers create personalized deadlines based on a contact's interaction with your email. They are useful for evergreen promotions, follow-up campaigns, and other offers where each contact should receive their own time-sensitive window instead of sharing one fixed expiration date.


A Dynamic Countdown Timer begins based on the trigger selected during setup:

  • Email Sent: The countdown is based on when the email is sent to the contact.

  • Email Opened: The countdown begins when the contact first opens the email.


For example, if you create a 2-hour Dynamic Timer using the Email Opened trigger:

  • A contact who opens the email at 2:00 PM sees a countdown that runs from 2:00 PM to 4:00 PM.

  • A contact who opens the same email at 5:00 PM sees a countdown that runs from 5:00 PM to 7:00 PM.


Dynamic Timers are designed to provide each contact with their own countdown experience while allowing you to reuse the same timer configuration in a campaign.

Key Benefits of Dynamic Countdown Timers


Dynamic Countdown Timers give you more control over personalized, time-sensitive campaigns without requiring a separate timer for every recipient.

  • Personalized timing: Each contact can receive a countdown based on their own email interaction rather than a single universal deadline.

  • Flexible triggers: Start the countdown based on when an email is sent or when the contact opens it.

  • Custom expiration behavior: Let the timer expire after a specific duration or combine the duration with an
    Ends at Time setting.

  • Cross-channel consistency: Dynamic Timers can be used with supported emails, funnels, and websites to help keep countdown experiences aligned.

  • Timezone flexibility: Countdown deadlines can adapt to contact or visitor timezones when timezone adaptation is enabled.

Dynamic Timer Triggers


The trigger determines when MxP begins calculating a contact's personalized countdown. Choosing the correct trigger is important because it defines when the contact's available time begins.


Email Sent


Use Email Sent when the countdown should begin as soon as the email is sent to the contact.


This option works well when every recipient should receive the same amount of time from the moment the message is delivered into the sending workflow.


Example:


You configure a 24-hour timer using Email Sent.

  • Contact A is sent the email at 8:00 AM. Their timer expires 24 hours later.

  • Contact B is sent the email at 11:00 AM. Their timer expires 24 hours after their individual send time.


Email Opened


Use Email Opened when you want the countdown to begin only after the contact opens the email. This is useful for evergreen offers where each contact should receive the full promotional window regardless of when they first engage with the message.


Example:


You configure a 2-hour timer using Email Opened.

  • Contact A opens the email at 1:00 PM. Their timer begins at 1:00 PM.

  • Contact B opens the email at 4:30 PM. Their timer begins at 4:30 PM.


A Dynamic Timer triggered by an email open is triggered once per contact. Reopening the email does not restart the countdown; subsequent opens display the remaining time from the original trigger.

Duration and Ends at Time


Duration controls the minimum or total countdown window, while the optional Ends at Time setting lets you align the countdown with a selected clock time. Understanding the difference helps you choose whether an offer should expire after a personalized interval or at a more structured time of day.


Duration Only


Disable Set End Time when you want the timer to expire strictly after the duration you configure.


For example, with a 2-hour duration and an Email Opened trigger:

  • A contact opening the email at 2:00 PM expires at 4:00 PM.

  • A contact opening it at 5:00 PM expires at 7:00 PM.


This option is useful when every contact should receive the same amount of time after triggering the timer. MxP documents this behavior as the Optional End Time configuration for Dynamic Timers.


Duration With Ends at Time


Enable the end-time option when the countdown should align with a selected clock time rather than ending solely according to the exact trigger timestamp. This can be useful for promotions that need a personalized activation point while still aligning expiration with a daily deadline.


Example:


You configure:

  • Trigger: Email Opened

  • Duration: 2 hours

  • Ends at Time: 11:00 PM


A contact who opens the email earlier in the day can count down toward the configured 11:00 PM deadline. If the minimum duration cannot be satisfied before that day's end time, the timer can use the next applicable occurrence of the selected end time.

Timezone Adaptation for Dynamic Timers


Timezone settings determine which local clock is used when a countdown relies on a specific end time. Timezone Adaptation is especially useful for campaigns that reach contacts in multiple regions.


When Adapt to Contact Time Zone is enabled:

  • Email timers use the contact's stored timezone when available.

  • Funnels and websites use the visitor's IP-detected timezone.

  • If MxP cannot determine the contact or visitor timezone, the timezone selected during timer setup is used as the fallback.


For example, if a campaign is designed to end at midnight in each recipient's local timezone, timezone adaptation helps ensure contacts in different regions see the appropriate localized countdown.

Using Dynamic Timers in Emails, Funnels, and Websites


Dynamic Timers can support personalized campaigns across multiple MxP experiences. Using the same timer with connected campaign content can help keep the deadline presented to a contact aligned between an email and its destination experience.

Emails


Dynamic Timers are especially useful when:

  • Every contact should receive their own countdown window.

  • The deadline depends on when an email is sent or opened.

  • The campaign is evergreen rather than tied to one fixed calendar date.

  • The email timer needs to remain aligned with a destination-page timer.

Active and Expired Links


Active and Expired links let you control where contacts are directed depending on whether their personalized countdown is still running. Setting these destinations correctly helps prevent contacts from accessing an expired offer after their timer reaches zero.

  • Active Link: The destination used while the offer or countdown is still active.

  • Expired Link: The destination used after the countdown has expired.


MxP can also generate a timer redirection link that routes contacts according to the timer's current state in supported contexts.


Use an Active Link for the valid promotional page and an Expired Link for a page explaining that the offer has ended or directing the contact to another available option.

How to Set Up a Dynamic Countdown Timer


Proper timer setup ensures each contact receives the intended countdown window, expiration behavior, timezone treatment, and destination. Review the trigger and expiration settings carefully before adding the timer to a live campaign.

  1. Navigate to Marketing > Countdown Timers.

  2. Click + New or the option to create a new countdown timer.


  3. Choose the countdown timer design you want to use and continue to the timer settings.

  4. Select Dynamic as the Timer Type.




  5. Select the appropriate trigger:

    • Email Sent to begin the countdown when the email is sent.

    • Email Opened to begin the countdown when the contact first opens the email.

  6. Set the Duration for the countdown.

    For example, choose 2 hours if each contact should receive at least or exactly a 2-hour window depending on the end-time configuration.

  7. Configure the end-time behavior:

    • Disable Set End Time if the timer should expire after the configured duration.

    • Enable the end-time setting if the countdown should align with a selected clock time.


  8. Select the appropriate Time Zone.

  9. Enable Adapt to Contact Time Zone when the countdown should adapt to the recipient or visitor's timezone. If timezone data is unavailable, MxP uses the timezone selected during setup as the fallback.

  10. Configure the Active Link and Expired Link.



  11. Customize the timer's appearance, including available font, color, sizing, labels, and displayed time units.



  12. Give the timer a descriptive name and save it.

  13. Add the timer to the appropriate email, funnel, website, or supported external implementation.

  1. Test the campaign before sending it to contacts. Confirm that:

  • The correct trigger is configured.

  • The timer duration matches the offer.

  • End-time and timezone behavior are correct.

  • Active and expired destinations work as expected.

Important Considerations for Dynamic Timers


Understanding how Dynamic Timers behave after activation helps prevent unintended extensions, reuse issues, and inconsistent recipient experiences.


Dynamic Timers Are Triggered Once Per Contact


A Dynamic Timer is designed for one-time use per contact. Once that contact triggers the timer, reopening the same email does not restart the countdown.

If you need to use a Dynamic Timer for the same contact in another campaign, MxP recommends cloning the timer before reuse.


Email Clients Can Affect Timer Display

Countdown timers in emails are rendered as dynamic image content. Some email clients may cache that content, which can affect how frequently the visible countdown refreshes.


Apple Mail, in particular, can cache timer images and may display an older timer state on subsequent opens. Test important campaigns across supported email clients before launch.


Test Expiration Behavior Before Launch


Always verify both the active and expired states. A countdown can appear correctly while still directing contacts to the wrong destination after expiration if the links are not configured correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions


Q: What happens if a contact never opens an email when Email Opened is the trigger?

The countdown does not begin from the Email Opened trigger until the contact performs the qualifying open interaction.


Q: Does reopening an email restart a Dynamic Timer?

No. MxP documents Dynamic Timers as one-time triggers per contact. After the first qualifying trigger, subsequent opens display the remaining time rather than restarting the countdown.


Q: Can I make the timer expire exactly after the configured duration?

Yes. Disable the end-time option. The Dynamic Timer will then expire after the configured duration from the applicable Email Sent or Email Opened trigger.


Q: Which timezone does a Dynamic Timer use?

When timezone adaptation is enabled, email timers use the contact's timezone when available, while funnels and websites use the visitor's IP-detected timezone. The timezone selected during timer setup acts as the fallback when timezone information is unavailable.


Q: Can I reuse the same Dynamic Timer for the same contact in another campaign?

MxP recommends cloning the timer before using it again for the same contact in another campaign because Dynamic Timers are one-time use per contact.


Q: Can I use a Dynamic Timer on a funnel or website?

Yes. Countdown Timers can be added to MxP funnels and websites. For the complete placement workflow, see How to Use Countdown Timers in Funnels.


Q: Can I embed a Dynamic Timer outside MxP?

Yes, using the Copy Code feature in supported external websites or email platforms. Dynamic Timers have additional implementation requirements, including a unique identifier and duration, so review the dedicated Copy Code documentation before embedding them externally.


Q: Why might a timer appear frozen in Apple Mail?

Apple Mail can cache timer image content. This may cause a previously loaded timer image to appear again instead of loading the latest visible countdown state.


Q: What happens when the timer expires?

The timer enters its expired state. The Expired Link can be used to direct the contact to the appropriate post-expiration destination.


Q: Should I use a Dynamic Timer or a Fixed Timer?

Use a Dynamic Timer when each contact should receive a personalized deadline based on their own interaction. Use a Fixed Timer when everyone should count down toward the same specific date and time.
