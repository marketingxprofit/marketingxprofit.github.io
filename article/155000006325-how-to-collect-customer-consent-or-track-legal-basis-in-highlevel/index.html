How to Collect Customer Consent or Track Legal Basis in MxP

MxP provides tools to collect customer consent, document a lawful basis for processing personal data, and manage communication preferences. This guide explains how consent differs from other lawful bases and how to configure consent collection in forms, surveys, chat widgets, calendars, and order forms. You'll also learn how to maintain consent evidence, use workflows responsibly, and handle withdrawals or marketing opt-outs. These features can support your privacy program but do not automatically establish legal compliance.

Important: This article provides general information, not legal advice. Your business must identify the requirements that apply to each processing activity. Adding a checkbox, tag, or custom field does not by itself make your business compliant. Consult your legal or privacy adviser when determining consent language and lawful bases.

What Is Customer Consent and Lawful Basis in MxP?

Customer consent is an affirmative choice that allows a business to process personal data for a clearly explained purpose. A lawful basis is the legal justification for a particular processing activity; consent is one possible basis, but it is not always the right one. MxP offers collection tools, contact fields, tags, and workflows that can help businesses document their decisions and honor customer preferences.

Key Benefits of Consent and Lawful Basis Tracking

Collecting clear permissions and maintaining reliable records helps your team understand which communications and data uses are appropriate for each contact. A consistent process also makes it easier to review your choices and respond when customer preferences change.

  • Clearer Customer Choices: Present purpose-specific disclosures and optional consent checkboxes wherever customers submit information.

  • Consistent Evidence: Keep relevant contact fields, submission records, and disclosure versions together in a documented process.

  • Connected Collection Points: Apply suitable consent settings across forms, surveys, chat widgets, calendars, and order forms.

  • Safer Automation: Use workflow conditions to distinguish an affirmative opt-in from an ordinary form submission.

  • Better Preference Management: Track withdrawals and apply communication restrictions for the affected channels.

Personal Data and GDPR Lawful Bases

Before adding consent checkboxes, determine what personal data your business processes and why. A documented lawful basis should match each processing purpose, rather than treating every submitted form or booking as permission for every future use.

Personal data is information about an identified or identifiable individual. Examples include names, email addresses, phone numbers, IP addresses, location data, payment details, and order history.

Under the GDPR, the six lawful bases for processing are:

  • Consent

  • Contract

  • Legal obligation

  • Vital interests

  • Public task

  • Legitimate interests

For example, fulfilling an order and sending optional marketing have different purposes and may rely on different legal grounds. A purchase or appointment booking does not automatically authorize unrelated marketing. Messaging, calling, and cookie rules may also impose separate requirements. For broader guidance, see the European Data Protection Board's lawful-processing guidance.

Controller and Processor Responsibilities

The roles of controller and processor determine who decides why personal data is used and who handles that information on another party's behalf. Knowing your role helps you assign responsibility for privacy notices, lawful bases, retention decisions, and customer requests.

RoleResponsibility
ControllerDetermines the purpose and lawful basis for processing personal data.
ProcessorProcesses personal data on a controller's behalf and according to applicable instructions.

Your business or client typically determines the purpose and lawful basis when acting as a controller. For customer personal data, MxP acts as a processor, or as a sub-processor when your agency processes information for a client, under its Customer Data Processing Addendum (DPA).

Publish your own privacy notice and make it accessible wherever you collect personal information. MxP's Privacy Policy addresses MxP's own activities, including account administration and website use; it does not replace your business's customer-facing notice.

Requirements for Valid Consent

When consent is your chosen GDPR lawful basis, the customer must have enough information to make a real, affirmative decision. Build these conditions into your collection experience and retain evidence that demonstrates what the person agreed to.

  • Explain the purpose: Identify your business, the intended data use, and relevant communication channels in clear language.

  • Require an affirmative action: Leave consent boxes unchecked. Preselected boxes, silence, or inactivity do not establish valid GDPR consent.

  • Provide a genuine choice: Keep optional marketing consent separate from terms acceptance or a service that does not require marketing.

  • Keep supporting evidence: Record what was agreed to, when and how the choice was made, and any later changes.

  • Allow withdrawal: Tell people how to withdraw and make withdrawal as easy as giving consent.

A privacy-policy link provides information, but clicking it or accepting general terms does not automatically authorize every processing purpose. See the European Data Protection Board's guidance on valid consent.

How to Set Up Customer Consent in MxP

Different MxP tools display consent requests and store related details in different ways. Configuring each collection point correctly helps you show the appropriate disclosure, record an affirmative response where required, and avoid assuming that ordinary form submission equals marketing consent.

Before You Begin

Prepare the privacy information and processing rules before editing individual forms or widgets. This reduces inconsistent disclosures when the same contact interacts with multiple pages or channels.

  1. Identify the personal data, processing purpose, and lawful basis for each customer touchpoint.

  2. Publish an accessible privacy notice for your business. See How to Link Your Privacy Policy When Collecting Personal Data.

  3. Prepare separate, clear disclosures for optional marketing or other processing that requires consent.

  4. Decide how you will retain consent evidence and honor later withdrawals.

Forms and Surveys

Forms and surveys can collect purpose-specific consent through a checkbox linked to a contact custom field. Testing both selected and unselected submissions helps confirm that the saved contact value reflects the customer's actual choice.

  1. Open the relevant Form Builder or Survey Builder.

  2. Add a Custom Checkbox field for the consent you intend to collect.

  3. Write the disclosure in clear language and include an accessible link to your business's privacy notice.

  4. Leave the checkbox unchecked by default. Do not make optional marketing consent a condition of submitting an unrelated service request.

  5. Save and publish, then submit test entries with and without consent. Review the stored field in the contact record.

Use separate choices when purposes or communication channels require distinct consent. Review embedded copies of forms and surveys as well as the standalone versions. For additional examples, see Gain Consent With Custom Checkboxes in Forms.

Example: an unchecked consent checkbox gives visitors the choice to opt in.

Chat Widget

Chat Widget compliance settings distinguish an informational legal notice from an affirmative checkbox. Configure these options separately so your visitor understands what information is collected and whether a particular choice is optional.

  1. Go to Sites > Chat Widget and open the widget you want to configure.

  2. Open Chat window > Additional options > Compliance notice & consent.

  3. Use Show legal notice to display a read-only disclosure; use Add a consent checkbox when an affirmative choice is needed.

  4. Review Make this checkbox required against the processing purpose. Leave optional marketing consent optional.

  5. Turn Pre-check the box by default off when relying on GDPR consent. Save and test the visitor experience.


Chat Widget submissions can record the consent response, whether the checkbox was required, and the text shown to the visitor in the contact's Activity details. Available settings depend on widget type. Widgets created for A2P registration may have locked disclosure or consent settings. See Overview of Chat Widget Customizations.


Calendars and Bookings

Calendar consent checkboxes allow contacts to acknowledge a customizable disclosure while booking an appointment. Configure the setting on each applicable calendar and check the stored details when you need to review a particular booking.

  1. Go to Calendars > Calendar Settings and edit the relevant calendar.

  2. Open Advanced Settings > Form & Confirmation.

  3. Enable Consent Checkbox and customize the wording for the intended purpose.

  4. Click Save Changes, then test the published booking flow.


To review consent, use View Consent from the applicable appointment's menu or open the appointment activity in the contact record. Details may include the accepted consent text and IP address. The native calendar checkbox does not support HTML or clickable links; place a privacy-policy link on the page containing the calendar or use an appropriate custom form. For images and more details, see How to Enable Consent Checkbox Calendars.

Order Forms, Funnels, and Websites

Order forms can display Terms & Conditions text and links, while funnels and websites often contain embedded forms, surveys, calendars, and chat widgets. Review every collection point so policy links and consent choices are appropriate to the specific action.

  1. Open the appropriate funnel or website checkout page and select its Order Form element.

  2. In the form settings, enable Terms and Conditions, customize the text, and add the applicable policy links.

  3. Collect optional marketing consent separately when it is required; acceptance of purchase terms is not by itself permission for unrelated marketing.

  4. Publish and test the page. Review embedded collection tools and any cookies or third-party tracking scripts used on the site.

For cookie choices on MxP funnels and websites, review the Cookie Consent Banner guide. Non-essential tracking should follow applicable consent requirements; a banner's appearance alone does not establish compliance.

Order Form Terms and Conditions settings. Obtain separate optional marketing consent when needed.

Track Lawful Basis and Consent Evidence

Documenting a lawful basis helps your team distinguish why each type of data is processed and what permissions currently apply. Contact custom fields and tags can support that recordkeeping, but they are not a built-in compliance register or a substitute for retaining the underlying evidence.

  1. Within the sub-account, go to Settings > Custom Fields and choose the Contact object.

  2. Create the fields needed to represent each purpose, lawful basis, consent status, source, and relevant dates.

  3. Optionally create tags for filtering by processing purpose or lawful basis.

  4. Review actual submissions, consent details, and disclosure versions to ensure the field values are supported.

These are suggested business-defined fields, not default MxP GDPR fields:

Suggested Contact FieldInformation to Record
Processing purpose and lawful basisThe specific use of data and its assessed basis; keep different purposes distinguishable.
Consent status by purpose or channelGranted, declined, withdrawn, or not recorded, as applicable.
Consent source and event dateThe actual form, booking, chat interaction, or other event and when the choice occurred.
Disclosure version or evidence referenceThe accepted wording or a reference to a retained copy and submission record.
Withdrawal date and scopeWhen consent was withdrawn and the activities or communication channels affected.

A tag alone does not prove consent, and a field showing only the latest value does not retain the complete change history. Keep supporting consent submissions, relevant native records, and disclosure versions according to your retention policy. See How to Use Custom Fields.

Use Workflows to Apply Consent Rules

Workflow conditions help prevent a form submission from being treated as an opt-in when the optional consent checkbox was not selected. Test the logic carefully, especially for existing contacts whose records might contain an older preference.

  1. Go to Automation > Workflows and create or edit a workflow.

  2. Add a Form Submitted trigger, filtered to the intended form.

  3. Add an If/Else condition that evaluates the relevant checkbox or contact field.

  4. On the affirmative branch, use Update Contact Field and appropriate tags to record the purpose, consent status, and supporting evidence reference.

  5. On the other branch, avoid starting marketing that requires the missing consent. Handle the underlying service request according to its separately assessed lawful basis.

  6. Test new contacts, existing contacts, repeat submissions, and consent-withdrawal scenarios before publishing the workflow.


Important: A form submission alone is not proof that an optional checkbox was checked. Confirm whether new submissions overwrite or preserve existing values, and do not mistake an older opt-in for a new choice. Do not automatically clear DND or override a recorded withdrawal without a verified, applicable opt-in. A workflow run time may be later than the actual consent event.

For action behavior and supported field types, see Workflow Action: Update Contact Field.

Handle Consent Withdrawals and Marketing Opt-outs

A consent process must account for what happens after someone changes their mind. Record the new preference, stop the processing covered by the withdrawal, and apply communication settings consistently with the scope of the request.

  1. Provide an accessible opt-out or withdrawal route appropriate to the channel and purpose.

  2. Record the withdrawal date, affected purpose, and supporting request details.

  3. Where appropriate, enable outbound Do Not Disturb (DND) for affected communication channels and remove the contact from marketing workflows.

  4. Review connected services, future scheduled sends, and other records that may need to reflect the same preference.

DND manages communications; it does not delete personal data or stop all processing. Some transactional emails, including payment invoices, calendar notifications, and certain notifications, may still be delivered despite Email DND. A marketing opt-out is different from a data erasure request. For channel-specific behavior, see How to Use Do Not Disturb (DND).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I require someone to accept optional marketing before booking an appointment?

A: Generally, optional marketing should not be a condition of obtaining an unrelated service. Keep the booking request and marketing preference separate, and have counsel review any requirement that may affect consent validity.

Q: How should I record consent collected outside MxP?

A: Record the source, purpose, actual event date, and a reference to the original disclosure and evidence. A copied value or tag in MxP does not create consent that was never obtained.

Q: Should email, SMS, and phone preferences be stored together?

A: Track distinct purposes and channels separately when the disclosures or applicable requirements differ. A contact may agree to one type of communication without agreeing to every other type.

Q: What if an existing contact submits a form again without selecting the optional checkbox?

A: Check the new submission and the stored contact value before updating the preference. An older selected field is not proof that the contact opted in on the newer submission; test repeat-submission behavior carefully.

Q: What if I change the wording of my consent request?

A: Retain the disclosure version associated with earlier choices and assess whether the changed purpose or wording requires a fresh consent request. Avoid rewriting past evidence to match the newest text.

Q: Can a previous opt-in automatically override a later unsubscribe or DND setting?

A: No. Respect the later withdrawal or opt-out unless you have verified a subsequent, applicable opt-in and the relevant channel rules permit a change. Do not clear DND solely because an older consent tag remains on the contact.
