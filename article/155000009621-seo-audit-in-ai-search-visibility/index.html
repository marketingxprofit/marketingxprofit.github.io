SEO Audit in AI & Search Visibility

Running a site audit helps you understand your website's technical SEO health and identify issues that may affect its visibility in search engines. AI & Search Visibility automatically starts your first SEO audit when you create a project in MxP. Once the scan finishes, you can review your website's health score, identify errors and warnings, and decide which improvements to make first.

What is a SEO Audit in AI & Search Visibility?


A Site Audit scans your website to identify technical SEO issues that may affect how search engines crawl, understand, and access your pages. It provides a website health score out of 100, a breakdown of detected issues, and recommendations for improving your site's technical health.


The first audit establishes a starting point for your website. After addressing the findings, you can run another scan to check whether the changes have improved your results.

What Does the Site SEO Audit Check?


A complete SEO audit examines several aspects of your website that influence how search engines discover, access, and interpret its content. Understanding these areas helps you recognize why particular issues appear in your report.


The audit includes:


Audit AreaWhat It Checks
Pages Needing AttentionIdentifies website pages with issues that may require improvements.
Indexability and RedirectsReviews whether pages can be indexed and identifies redirect-related issues.
Link DiscoveryExamines links that help search engines discover and navigate website pages.
Core Web VitalsReviews performance indicators related to loading speed, responsiveness, and visual stability.
Crawled ResourcesExamines resources discovered during the crawl, such as scripts, stylesheets, and other page assets.


The report also includes individual SEO action items with recommendations for addressing identified issues.

How to Run Your First Site Audit?


Your first site audit starts automatically when you create a project in AI & Search Visibility. Opening the SEO Audit module allows you to review the scan's progress and access the results once they are ready.


Follow these steps:


  1. Open AI & Search Visibility.

  2. Create a new project by entering your business information and website domain, then completing the project setup.

    Note: If you already created a project, you do not need to create another one. Open your existing project to access its audit results.

  3. Once the project is created, the first SEO audit starts automatically. (After the initial scan, SEO Audits do not run automatically. To check for new issues or review updated results, users must manually initiate subsequent audits)

  4. Open SEO Audit from the side navigation panel.

  5. Allow the scan to finish. The initial audit generally takes a few minutes, depending on the website being scanned.

    When the audit is complete, review the report displaying your Website Health Score, Errors, Warnings, and Notices.

You can now begin reviewing the issues identified during the scan.


Tip: Ask Visibility also includes a Run SEO Audit suggestion. However, you do not need to use it to initiate your first scan because the initial audit starts automatically during project creation.


Understanding Your First Site Audit Results


The completed audit provides an overview of your website's technical health and the issues detected during scanning. The score and issue counts help you understand the starting condition of your website before making improvements.


Website Health Score


The Health Score is a single number from 0 to 100 that summarizes the technical health of your website. A higher score means fewer and less serious issues were found when we audited your site.

How is it calculated?

During the audit, we crawl your website’s pages and run more than 100 technical checks, grouped into areas such as Crawling & Indexing, Security, Redirects, Content, Meta Tags, Speed & Performance, and Mobile Optimization. The score is then based on three things:


  • How serious each issue is. Every issue has a priority: Errors are critical problems, Warnings are moderate problems, and Notices are minor or informational items. Errors lower your score the most, while Notices have little or no impact.

  • How many of your pages are affected. An issue that appears on most of your pages lowers the score more than one that affects only a few.

  • Which area the issue belongs to. Some areas matter more for search visibility than others. For example, problems that stop search engines from crawling or indexing your pages carry more weight than cosmetic issues.

How to improve your score
Start with Errors, especially those affecting many pages, then work through Warnings. After you fix them, run the audit again to see your updated score.

Good to know


  • The score reflects your site at the time of the last audit, so it changes as you fix issues or publish new pages.

  • Treat it as a directional indicator. A sudden drop is a signal to review recent changes on your site.


Important: The health score is not a search engine ranking or a guarantee of organic traffic. It reflects the technical issues evaluated during the audit.


Errors, Warnings, and Notices


The audit groups detected issues by severity to help you understand their importance.


SeverityWhat It Means
ErrorsHigher-severity issues that generally deserve attention first.
WarningsPotential SEO problems that should be reviewed and addressed where relevant.
NoticesLower-severity findings or opportunities for improvement.


An individual website page can have more than one issue. As a result, the total number of errors, warnings, and notices may exceed the number of URLs scanned.


SEO Audit Beta Limits


The beta includes a monthly audit allowance for each project. Understanding this limit helps you plan additional scans and avoid using them before you've had an opportunity to address identified issues.


  • Each project receives 10 SEO audits per monthly cycle during the beta.

  • Once the limit is reached, the latest completed audit report remains available.

  • You can continue reviewing that report and working on its recommended fixes even when no additional audits are available.


Tip: Review your first audit, make the necessary changes, and then rescan to assess improvements rather than running repeated scans without addressing the findings. 

After the initial scan, SEO Audits do not run automatically. To check for new issues or review updated results, users must manually initiate subsequent audits.
