How to Link Your Privacy Policy When Collecting Personal Data

A privacy policy, also called a privacy notice, explains how your business collects, uses, discloses, retains, and protects personal information. Making your policy accessible wherever you collect customer data helps visitors understand your practices before they submit information. MxP supports privacy-policy links across forms, surveys, funnels, websites, calendars, order forms, chat widgets, and ecommerce checkout. Use this guide to publish your policy, configure each collection point, and verify that visitors can open the right notice.

Important: This article provides general information, not legal advice. Your privacy notice must reflect your actual business practices and applicable requirements. Adding a link or checkbox does not automatically establish compliance or consent for every use of personal data.

What Is a Privacy Policy in MxP?

A privacy policy is your business's public explanation of how it handles personal information. In MxP, you can publish a policy page and place links at the points where visitors provide contact, booking, or payment information. The notice should identify the responsible business and accurately describe its actual practices; MxP's own Privacy Policy does not replace it.

Key Benefits of Linking Your Privacy Policy

Consistent, accessible policy links make it easier for visitors to review your data practices before sharing their information. Using the right placement for each collection tool also helps your team maintain notices across pages and client accounts.

  • Clear Visitor Information: Give people a direct way to understand how their personal data is handled before submitting it.

  • Consistent Collection Experience: Make privacy notices available across supported MxP forms, surveys, bookings, chats, and checkouts.

  • Flexible Policy Hosting: Publish a dedicated page in MxP or link to an existing public privacy-policy page.

  • Better Policy Maintenance: Use stable, tested links and keep prior notice versions when your business practices change.

  • Separate Consent Choices: Keep required policy information distinguishable from optional marketing permissions and other consent requests.

Before You Begin

Identify each place where your business collects personal information, including embedded forms and third-party pages. Prepare a notice that matches those activities and is accessible before people submit their information.

For processing covered by the GDPR, review the applicable transparency requirements, including:

  • Your business's identity and contact details, and representative or data protection officer details where applicable.

  • The personal data processed, purposes, lawful bases, and legitimate interests where relevant.

  • Recipients or recipient categories, international transfers, and applicable safeguards.

  • Retention periods or the criteria used to determine them.

  • Applicable rights, how to exercise them, consent withdrawal where relevant, and the right to complain to a supervisory authority.

  • Required information and consequences of not providing it, and relevant automated decision-making details where applicable.

Additional disclosures may apply when information comes from another source or under other privacy laws. See GDPR Articles 12–14 and have your legal or privacy adviser review your notice.

For U.S. business messaging: A2P 10DLC carrier and registration requirements are separate from privacy laws. Review the applicable MxP A2P opt-in requirements for public policy links, business information, and messaging disclosures.

How to Set Up and Publish Your Privacy Policy Page

Before you add policy links to your collection points, publish a complete, accessible privacy notice for the correct business. MxP can host the page within a funnel or website, or you can link to an existing public policy.

  1. Go to Sites > Funnels or Sites > Websites.

  2. Open the appropriate funnel or website and add a page titled Privacy Policy.

  3. Add your business's reviewed privacy-policy content using text elements.

  4. Publish the page and copy its public URL.

  5. Open the URL while signed out to confirm visitors can read it without account access.

Use a stable address that you can maintain. If your business has separate brands or clients, make sure each collection point links to the correct business's notice.


Where to Add Your Privacy Policy Link

Use clear link text such as Privacy Policy. Place the link near the relevant collection point so visitors can review it before submitting. A footer link can provide additional access.

Funnels and Websites

Funnels and websites often contain forms or booking tools that collect information directly from visitors. Place a nearby privacy-policy link so visitors can review your practices before submitting their details.

  1. Open the relevant page in the funnel or website builder.

  2. Add or select a text element near the form, booking widget, or other collection point.

  3. Enter a notice such as For details about how we use your information, read our Privacy Policy.
  4. Highlight Privacy Policy, select the link control, and paste your published policy URL.

  5. Save and publish. Repeat on the other relevant pages and embedded collection tools.


Forms

Forms can be embedded on multiple pages, so adding the link inside the form helps keep the notice alongside the fields where information is collected.

  1. Open the relevant form in the Form Builder.

  2. Add a Text element with your notice, highlight Privacy Policy, and link it to your published URL.

  3. If using a Terms & Conditions element, use Edit Link to configure the applicable policy link and review the displayed wording.

  4. Save and publish the form, then test both its public version and any embedded copies.


Keep an optional marketing checkbox separate from terms acceptance or an acknowledgment of your notice. For A2P opt-in forms, follow the applicable requirements for visible Privacy Policy and Terms & Conditions links in the form footer.

Surveys

Surveys may collect personal information through both contact fields and responses. A link within the survey makes the relevant notice available during completion.

  1. Open the survey in the Survey Builder.

  2. Add a Text element containing your privacy notice link.

  3. Highlight Privacy Policy, select the link control, and enter the published URL.

  4. Save, publish, and check that visitors can access the notice before submitting personal data.


Calendars

Calendar bookings need an accessible policy link even though the native consent-checkbox text cannot contain clickable links. Use the page surrounding the calendar or an appropriate custom form.

The native calendar consent-checkbox text does not support HTML or clickable links. Use one of these supported approaches:

  • Add a text link to the page where the calendar is embedded, positioned so visitors can review it before booking.

  • Use an appropriate custom booking form with a Terms & Conditions element containing the policy link.

If you use a custom form, verify that it is assigned to each intended calendar. Test the actual booking link you share with customers; a link on an embedding page is not automatically present in a standalone calendar.

Calendar consent settings are separate from policy-link placement. See How to Enable Consent Checkbox Calendars.


Order Forms

Order forms often require acceptance of purchase terms in addition to collecting customer details. Place your privacy notice where buyers can read it, without treating terms acceptance as optional marketing consent.

  1. Open the funnel or website page containing your order form.

  2. Add a text link to your privacy policy near the order form.

  3. If using the order form's Terms and Conditions option, open its advanced settings, enable the option, and configure the applicable text and links.

  4. Save, publish, and test the checkout experience.

Acceptance of purchase terms does not automatically provide consent for unrelated marketing. Configure any required marketing opt-in separately.

Chat Widget

Chat widgets can collect contact details before a conversation starts. Configure the informational legal notice and any required consent choice separately.

  1. Go to Sites > Chat Widget and open the relevant widget.

  2. Open Chat window > Additional options > Compliance notice & consent.

  3. Use Show legal notice to display an informational disclosure. Add your privacy-policy link in the supported disclosure or description editor using its link control.

  4. If consent is needed, configure Add a consent checkbox separately. Leave Pre-check the box by default off when relying on GDPR consent.

  5. Save and test the widget on your published site.

Available settings depend on widget type. A2P-generated widgets may have locked disclosures or consent settings. Confirm that the public widget displays a working policy link. See Overview of Chat Widget Customizations.


Ecommerce Store Checkout

Store checkout can display a link to your privacy notice within its terms text. Check that the notice remains accessible to customers before they complete payment.

  1. Go to Sites > Stores, open your store, and edit the Checkout page.

  2. Select the Checkout element.

  3. In the right panel, find Terms and Conditions and turn on Enable Terms and Conditions if you require acceptance.

  4. Customize the checkbox text and add the applicable Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy links.

  5. Save and publish, then test on desktop and mobile.

When enabled, the checkout checkbox is unchecked by default and must be selected before payment. This checkout acceptance is separate from optional marketing consent.

See Ecommerce Store: Customizing Checkout Pages.

Keep Privacy Notices and Consent Separate

A privacy notice explains your practices. A lawful basis justifies a particular processing activity. Consent is one possible basis and, when used under the GDPR, must be freely given, specific, informed, and affirmative.

Use informational wording for the policy link, such as Read our Privacy Policy to learn how we handle your information. Avoid treating a statement such as By submitting, you agree to our Privacy Policy as blanket consent.

Where consent is required, provide a separate, unchecked choice for the relevant purpose. Keep optional marketing optional and explain how to withdraw. Retain the appropriate consent evidence and disclosure version.

Cookie and tracking consent also needs its own configuration. Linking a policy does not control whether analytics or advertising scripts run. See How to Use the Cookie Consent Banner.

Use MxP's Privacy and Security Documents

Your business's notice should explain its actual practices, including its use of MxP and other providers. Use these documents to inform that review:

  • Privacy Policy: Explains MxP's own controller activities. It does not replace your business's customer-facing notice.

  • Customer DPA: Describes MxP's processing of customer personal data, including processor or sub-processor roles and contractual safeguards.

  • Sub-Processors: Identifies listed providers, activities, and processing locations. Review it when assessing recipients and international transfers, together with your own integrations.

  • Privacy & Security Center: Provides current security and assurance resources. Describe only the safeguards that accurately apply to your own operations.

Review these sources when your services, providers, tracking, AI features, retention practices, or processing purposes change. Do not copy MxP's policy or security claims as if they describe your business.

Test and Maintain Your Links

Checking each published collection point helps catch broken, hidden, or outdated policy links before they affect visitors. Test both standalone pages and embedded experiences, including mobile layouts, whenever you make changes.

  • Open each published form, survey, booking page, chat widget, order form, and store checkout while signed out.

  • Confirm the link is visible before submission, including on mobile and within embedded tools.

  • Click the link and verify it opens the correct business's complete, published notice without a login.

  • Check that pop-ups, banners, or layout elements do not obscure the link.

  • Test any required terms checkbox and optional consent choices independently.

  • Keep earlier notice versions and effective dates according to your recordkeeping needs. Update affected links if the URL changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I link to MxP's Privacy Policy instead of my own?

A: MxP's policy does not replace a notice describing your business's collection and use of customer information. Publish the notice appropriate to your business and link to it.

Q: Do I need a separate website to host my policy?

A: No. You can publish a dedicated policy page in a MxP funnel or website, or use another publicly accessible host.

Q: Can I put a clickable link in the native calendar consent text?

A: No. Use the page containing the calendar or a suitable custom form with a policy link. Test the exact booking experience customers will use.

Q: Does accepting checkout terms mean a customer opted into marketing?

A: Not automatically. Keep terms acceptance, privacy information, and optional marketing choices distinguishable and apply the requirements for each purpose.
