Understanding Your Subscription Invoice and Prorated Charges

When you make changes to your subscription during an active billing cycle, your next invoice may include additional charges or credits. This can make the total amount look different from your regular monthly subscription price.

This guide explains how subscription billing works, how prorated charges are calculated, and how to read your invoice so you know exactly what you're paying for.

How Does Subscription Billing Work?

Subscriptions are billed in advance for each billing cycle. When you purchase a subscription, you're charged immediately, and your subscription becomes active.

If you make changes during the billing cycle, such as adding additional seats, those changes are reflected in your next invoice.

Here's how the billing process works:

ActionBilling Behavior
Purchase a subscriptionYou're charged immediately, and the subscription starts right away.
Add seats during a billing cycleThe additional cost is prorated based on the remaining days and included in your next invoice.
Renew your subscriptionYou're charged upfront for the next billing cycle based on your current seat count.
Cancel your subscriptionYour subscription remains active until the end of the paid billing cycle. No further renewal charges are made, and the current cycle is not refunded.

Important: Changes made during an active billing cycle are not charged immediately. Instead, the prorated adjustment is included in your next invoice.


What Are Prorated Charges?

Proration means you're charged only for the portion of a billing cycle during which a subscription change was active.

For example, suppose you're paying $10 per month for one seat. Halfway through your billing cycle, you add a second seat.

Since you've already paid for the month with one seat, the system calculates:

  • Unused time: A credit for the remaining days at your previous seat count.

  • Remaining time: A charge for the same remaining days at your new seat count.

The difference between these amounts is the actual cost of your subscription change.


Example: Adding a Seat Mid-Cycle

Let's say:

  • Your subscription costs $10 per seat per month.

  • You initially have 1 seat.

  • On January 16, you increase your subscription to 2 seats.

  • Your next billing cycle begins on January 31.

Your next invoice includes:

DescriptionAmount
Remaining time for 2 seats (Jan 16–31)$10.00
Credit for unused time on 1 seat (Jan 16–31)-$5.00
Net cost of adding the seat$5.00
Regular subscription for 2 seats (Jan 31–Feb 28)$20.00
Total amount due$25.00

The additional $5 is not a duplicate charge. It represents the cost of adding a second seat for the remaining portion of the previous billing cycle.

How to Read Your Subscription Invoice

Your subscription invoice contains the charges, credits, and subscription details for your billing cycle.

Understanding the different sections can help you identify what you're being charged for.

Understanding Remaining Time and Unused Time

When you change your subscription mid-cycle, your invoice may display two adjustment line items.

1. Remaining Time

This is the charge for your updated subscription quantity over the remaining days of the billing cycle.

For example:

Remaining time on 2 seats after January 16: $10.00

2. Unused Time

This is the credit for the amount you've already paid for your previous subscription quantity over those same days.

For example:

Unused time on 1 seat after January 16: -$5.00

These two entries represent a single subscription change.

Net adjustment = Remaining Time Charge − Unused Time Credit

In this example:

$10.00 − $5.00 = $5.00

Important: A negative amount on your invoice represents a credit that reduces what you owe. It is not an additional charge or a separate refund.

How Is the Total Calculated?


Step 1: Calculate the charge for the new seat count.

For the remaining portion of the current billing cycle, the updated subscription quantity is charged $10.00.

Step 2: Apply the credit for the previous seat count.

Since you've already paid for one seat during that period, a $5.00 credit is applied.

Step 3: Calculate the net adjustment.

$10.00 − $5.00 = $5.00

Step 4: Add the upcoming month's subscription charge.

2 seats × $10.00 = $20.00

Step 5: Calculate the total invoice amount.

$5.00 + $20.00 = $25.00

Your invoice includes both the prorated adjustment from the previous billing cycle and the regular subscription charge for the upcoming month.

Why Is My Invoice Higher Than Expected?

If you've added seats or made other subscription changes during a billing cycle, your next invoice may be higher than your regular monthly subscription price.

This happens because the invoice combines two separate charges:

  1. Prorated adjustment: A one-time charge for changes made during the previous billing cycle.

  2. Regular subscription charge: The full amount for your upcoming billing cycle.

Example

DescriptionAmount
Expected monthly subscription (2 seats)$20.00
One-time prorated adjustment$5.00
Actual invoice total$25.00

The additional $5.00 is a one-time adjustment for the extra seat added during the previous cycle.


Will I Be Charged This Amount Every Month?

No. The prorated adjustment applies only to the billing cycle in which the subscription change occurred.

If you don't make any further changes, your following invoice will reflect your regular subscription amount.

In this example:

  • Current invoice: $25.00 ($20 subscription + $5 adjustment)

  • Following invoice: $20.00 (regular subscription only)

If you make another mid-cycle subscription change, a new prorated adjustment may appear on a future invoice.

How to Calculate Prorated Charges

Prorated charges are calculated based on your subscription price, the updated quantity, and the number of days remaining in your billing cycle.


Calculation Formula

Step 1: Calculate the daily rate.

Daily Rate = Monthly Price per Seat ÷ Days in Billing Cycle

Step 2: Calculate the remaining-time charge.

Remaining Time Charge = New Seat Count × Daily Rate × Days Remaining

Step 3: Calculate the unused-time credit.

Unused Time Credit = Old Seat Count × Daily Rate × Days Remaining

Step 4: Calculate the net adjustment.

Net Prorated Adjustment = Remaining Time Charge − Unused Time Credit


What Affects the Adjustment Amount?


The amount depends on when you make the change.

  • Earlier in the billing cycle: More days remaining means a larger prorated adjustment.

  • Later in the billing cycle: Fewer days remaining means a smaller prorated adjustment.

For example, adding a $10-per-month seat halfway through the billing cycle results in an approximately $5 adjustment.

The exact amount depends on the days remaining in your billing cycle.


Quick Way to Check Your Invoice


If your invoice includes a regular monthly subscription charge and a prorated adjustment, you can calculate the difference:

Total Invoice Amount − Regular Monthly Subscription Charge = Net Prorated Adjustment

For example:

$25.00 − $20.00 = $5.00

This helps you understand how much of your invoice comes from changes made during the previous billing cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why is there a negative amount on my invoice?

A negative amount represents a credit for unused subscription time.

When you change your seat count during a billing cycle, the system credits the amount you previously paid for the remaining days at your old seat count.

This credit is applied directly to your invoice and reduces your total amount due.


Q: Why do I see both "Remaining Time" and "Unused Time" on my invoice?

These two entries represent the same subscription change.

Remaining Time is the charge for your updated seat count, while Unused Time is the credit for the previous seat count.

Together, they calculate the actual cost of your change for the remaining portion of the billing cycle.


Q: Why is my invoice higher than my monthly subscription price?

Your invoice may include a one-time prorated adjustment for changes made during your previous billing cycle, in addition to your regular upcoming monthly subscription charge.

For example, a $20 monthly subscription with a $5 prorated adjustment results in a $25 invoice.


Q: Will the prorated adjustment appear every month?

No. A prorated adjustment is a one-time charge associated with a specific subscription change.

Your future invoices will reflect your regular subscription amount unless additional changes are made during a billing cycle.


Q: What happens if I add multiple seats at different times during the month?

Each subscription change generates its own prorated adjustment.

If you make multiple changes during the same billing cycle, your next invoice may include multiple pairs of Remaining Time and Unused Time entries.

Each pair corresponds to the date the change was made.


Q: Why does the seat count in my adjustment lines differ from my current subscription?

The adjustment lines show your seat count at the time of each subscription change.

If you made additional changes afterward, your regular monthly subscription charge will reflect your latest seat count at renewal.


Q: Am I charged immediately when I add another seat?

No. Under this subscription billing model, changes made during an active billing cycle are reflected as prorated adjustments on your next invoice.


Q: What happens if I cancel my subscription?

Your subscription remains active until the end of your already-paid billing cycle.

You will not receive a refund for the current cycle, but you will not be charged for another renewal after the subscription ends.


Q: How can I verify whether my invoice is correct?

Review the Remaining Time and Unused Time entries for each subscription change.

Add the adjustments together, then add your regular subscription charge for the upcoming billing cycle.

The total should match the amount shown on your invoice.


Q: What should I do if I don't recognize a charge?

Contact MxP Support and provide the invoice number displayed at the top of your invoice.

The support team can help review the individual line items and explain the charges.

Key Takeaways

  • Subscriptions are billed in advance for each billing cycle.

  • Mid-cycle subscription changes are prorated and reflected in the next invoice.

  • Remaining Time represents the charge for the updated subscription quantity.

  • Unused Time represents the credit for the previous subscription quantity.

  • The difference between these amounts is the net cost of the change.

  • Your invoice may include both a one-time prorated adjustment and the upcoming monthly subscription charge.

  • Prorated adjustments are not recurring charges unless additional subscription changes occur.

Understanding these line items can help you verify your invoice and distinguish regular subscription charges from one-time billing adjustments.
