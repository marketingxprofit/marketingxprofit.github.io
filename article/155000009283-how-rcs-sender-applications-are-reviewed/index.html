How RCS Sender Applications Are Reviewed
What internal and carrier reviewers check, how to fix common issues, and how long approval usually takes.
Overview

When you submit an RCS (Rich Communication Services) sender for approval, the registration goes through a review process to ensure that your business, messaging use case, and sender information meet the applicable compliance and carrier requirements. The review generally involves two main stages:

Stage 1 · Internal Review — our team checks the application for completeness, accuracy, and compliance.
Stage 2 · Carrier Review — carriers and applicable verification providers validate the brand, messaging use case, and compliance requirements before approving the sender.

Internal Review

After an RCS sender is submitted, an RCS onboarding specialist on our team reviews the application to ensure that all required information has been provided and that the submission complies with applicable messaging policies. Here's what internal reviewers check:

Check A
Business and Brand Verification

Reviewers verify that the sender represents a legitimate business. They check:

  • Legal business name and registration details
  • Official business website and contact information
  • Authorized representative details
  • Consistency between the brand name, website, and sender identity
  • Supporting business verification or attestation documents, where required
Check B
Sender Profile and Branding

Reviewers make sure the sender profile accurately represents the business. This includes:

  • Sender display name
  • Business description
  • Brand logo and banner
  • Business website
  • Privacy Policy and Terms of Service URLs

All branding details should consistently represent the same business, and the required URLs must be accessible.

Check C
Opt-In and Opt-Out Compliance

One of the most important parts of the review is verifying how customers consent to receive RCS messages. Reviewers check:

  • How customers provide consent
  • Whether the opt-in form is live and accessible
  • Whether the consent checkbox is unchecked by default, where applicable
  • Whether the consent language clearly identifies the business
  • Whether the types of messages customers will receive are explained
  • Whether customers can unsubscribe by replying STOP
  • Whether HELP and opt-out responses meet applicable requirements
  • Whether the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service are available
Important

Reviewers may test the opt-in process. The consent flow should be functional and accessible during the review.

Check D
Messaging Use Case and Sample Messages

Reviewers verify that the intended messaging activity complies with applicable policies. They examine:

  • Purpose of the messaging campaign
  • Types of messages being sent, such as promotional, transactional, or informational
  • Events that trigger messages
  • Sample messages
  • Expected message frequency
  • Whether sample messages match the submitted use case
Check E
Required Documentation

Depending on the country, additional documentation may be required. Examples include:

  • Business registration documents
  • Brand verification or attestation letters
  • Authorization documents
  • Screenshots of consent forms
  • Demonstration videos showing the opt-in and messaging experience
Note

If any information is missing or incorrect, our team may request corrections before proceeding with the registration.

Carrier Review

Once the application passes the internal review, it is submitted to the applicable carriers and verification providers. Carriers perform their own checks to confirm that the sender meets their requirements. Here's what carrier reviewers check:

Check A
Brand Legitimacy

Carriers verify that the business is legitimate and authorized to use the submitted sender identity. This may include:

  • Validating the legal business information
  • Confirming that the sender name matches the business brand
  • Reviewing business registration documents
  • Verifying brand ownership or authorization
US Carriers · Aegis Verification

For US carrier registration, Aegis, a third-party verification provider, may perform additional verification of the business and its authorized representative. Make sure the representative uses a company-branded email address and responds promptly to any verification email.

Check B
Messaging Use Case

Carriers review whether the intended messaging activity is permitted under their policies. They may check:

  • Campaign description
  • Intended audience
  • Promotional or transactional messaging purpose
  • Message triggers
  • Sample messages
  • Expected message frequency

The submitted information must accurately represent how the business intends to use RCS messaging.

Check C
Opt-In and Opt-Out Verification

Carriers may independently test the opt-in flow to verify that customers have explicitly consented to receive messages. They check:

  • Whether the opt-in process is functional
  • Whether the consent language clearly identifies the business
  • Whether the message purpose is explained
  • Whether the required disclosures are present
  • Whether STOP and HELP instructions are supported
  • Whether Privacy Policy and Terms of Service links are accessible

For US carriers, applicable CTIA messaging guidelines are also considered during compliance review.

Check D
Expected Messaging Volume

Some carriers may request information about anticipated messaging traffic. This can include:

  • Estimated monthly RCS message volume
  • Existing SMS messaging volume
  • Expected message frequency
  • Existing messaging campaigns
  • Business website traffic information
Check E
Additional Carrier-Specific Requirements

Depending on the country or carrier, additional verification may be necessary. Examples include:

  • Agent Verification Forms
  • Brand verification emails
  • Additional business documents
  • Authorization or attestation letters
  • Demonstration videos
  • Additional campaign information
Heads Up

Carriers may request further clarification before approving the sender.

What Happens If Reviewers Find an Issue?

If our team or the carriers identify missing information or compliance concerns, the application may require corrections before approval. Common issues include:

IssueRequired Action
Incorrect business informationUpdate the legal business details to match official records.
Sender name does not match the brandEnsure consistent branding across the registration and website.
Opt-in form is not accessibleProvide a working opt-in form.
Consent language is incompleteInclude the required brand identification, message purpose, and opt-out disclosures.
Privacy Policy or Terms of Service links are brokenProvide valid, accessible links.
Messaging use case is unclearSubmit a detailed use case and representative sample messages.
Brand verification is incompleteComplete the verification process or provide the required documentation.
Additional carrier documents are missingSubmit the requested documents.
Good to Know

Once the required corrections are completed, the application can continue through the review process.

4

How Long Does the Review Take?

RCS registration timelines depend on the destination country, carrier, and completeness of the submitted application.

US Timeline

For US RCS onboarding, plan for approximately 4–6 weeks or longer for the overall process.

The review may take longer if:

  • Business information needs correction.
  • The opt-in flow fails verification.
  • Additional documentation is requested.
  • Brand verification remains incomplete.
  • Carriers request clarification.
  • Carrier network freezes or major holidays affect processing.
Note

Submitting accurate and complete information can help reduce avoidable delays, but approval timelines are not guaranteed.

Checklist Before Submitting an RCS Sender

Before submitting your RCS registration, ensure the following requirements are met:

✓  Legal business name and registration details are accurate.
✓  Authorized representative information is correct.
✓  Sender name, logo, and website consistently represent the business.
✓  Privacy Policy and Terms of Service links are functional.
✓  Opt-in form is live and accessible.
✓  Consent language clearly explains the types of messages customers will receive.
✓  STOP and HELP instructions meet applicable requirements.
✓  Messaging use case and sample messages are accurate.
✓  Expected messaging volume is provided where required.
✓  All necessary business verification documents are available.
✓  Additional country-specific requirements have been completed.

Important Things to Remember

Keep in Mind
  • Our team reviews the application before submitting it for applicable carrier approval.
  • Passing the internal review does not guarantee carrier approval.
  • Carriers may request additional information or documentation.
  • A live and compliant opt-in process is essential.
  • Approval requirements vary depending on the destination country and carrier.
  • Google RCS approval does not automatically guarantee approval for every carrier or country.
You're All Set

Once the sender receives the required approvals and is activated, it can be used for RCS messaging on supported networks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between the internal review and the carrier review?
The internal review is done by our RCS onboarding team to confirm your application is complete, accurate, and compliant. The carrier review is done by the carriers and verification providers themselves, who make the final approval decision.
Q: If my application passes the internal review, will it be approved by carriers?
Not necessarily. Passing the internal review means your application is ready to be submitted, but carriers run their own independent checks and may request more information or decline the sender.
Q: How long does US RCS approval usually take?
Plan for approximately 4–6 weeks or longer. Corrections, extra documentation requests, carrier clarifications, network freezes, and holidays can all extend the timeline.
Q: Will reviewers actually test my opt-in form?
Yes, they may. Both internal reviewers and carriers can test the consent flow, so keep the opt-in form live and accessible throughout the review.
Q: What is Aegis verification?
For US carrier registration, Aegis is a third-party verification provider that may verify your business and its authorized representative(The brand's domain email should be used for this). Use a subaccount-branded email for the representative and respond promptly to any verification email.
Q: Why are carriers asking about my messaging volume?
Some carriers request estimated RCS volume, existing SMS volume, message frequency, and website traffic to understand the expected traffic and assess the sender. Provide realistic estimates.
Q: What happens if reviewers find a problem with my application?
You will be asked to make corrections or provide additional documents. Once the required changes are completed, the application continues through the review process. See Section 3 for common issues and fixes.
Q: Does Google RCS approval mean I can message every country?
No. Google approval does not automatically guarantee approval for every carrier or country. Additional country- or carrier-specific approvals may still be required.
